# CAET Micro-Lesson Template

> **Design Basis**: This template is grounded in Merrill's First Principles of Instruction, Mayer's Multimedia Learning Theory, and the NEETS (Navy Electricity and Electronics Training Series) progressive-disclosure format. Each micro-lesson targets a single learning objective and is designed to be completed in 3–7 minutes.

---

## Template Structure

Each node follows this exact 5-section structure:

### 1. 🎯 WHY THIS MATTERS (Activation)
> *Merrill: "Learning is promoted when learners are engaged in solving real-world problems."*

A 2–3 sentence scenario grounded in the hangar floor. This section answers: **"Why should I care about this right now?"** It connects the concept to a real consequence the apprentice could face on the job. No jargon. Written in second person ("You walk into the shop and notice...").

### 2. 📖 WHAT YOU NEED TO KNOW (Demonstration)
> *NEETS-style: Progressive disclosure. Start with the simplest form of the concept, then build.*

The core instructional content. Written in clear, direct, technical language appropriate for entry-level AMTs. Uses:
- **Bold** for key terms on first use
- Numbered lists for procedures/steps
- Bullet lists for requirements/criteria
- Short paragraphs (3–4 sentences max)
- No filler — every sentence must teach something assessable

### 3. 🔑 KEY TERMS
> *Mayer's Signaling Principle: "Learning is improved when cues highlight the essential material."*

A concise glossary of the critical terms from the lesson. Format:
- **Term** — Definition in one plain-language sentence.

### 4. 🔧 ON THE JOB (Application)
> *Merrill: "Learning is promoted when learners apply new knowledge to real-world situations."*

A brief (2–3 sentence) real-world scenario or practical rule-of-thumb that reinforces the concept. This is NOT a test question — it's a mental rehearsal. Written as: "When you are [doing X], remember to [do Y] because [consequence Z]."

### 5. ⚡ BOTTOM LINE (Integration)
> *One sentence. The single most important takeaway the student must remember.*

Bold. Direct. If the student remembers nothing else, they remember this.

---

## Quality Standards
- Every sentence in Section 2 must be traceable to an assessment question or learning objective
- No opinion, no conversational filler, no "In this lesson you will learn..."
- Technical accuracy: all procedures must align with 14 CFR Part 145 and industry standards
- Reading level: 8th–10th grade (Flesch-Kincaid)
- Length: 200–500 words per node (micro-lesson constraint)
