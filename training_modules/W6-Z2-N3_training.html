W6 // DATABUS // ARINC 429 ARCHITECTURE

ARINC 429 speed/timing awareness

Section 1.0 // Learning Objectives

Module Objectives

  • Differentiate between High Speed and Low Speed ARINC 429.
  • Explain the timing gaps between words.
Section 2.0 // Introduction

Why Does This Matter?

Connecting a 100kbps transmitter to a 12.5kbps receiver results in total data loss.

Section 3.0 // Core Principles

What You Need To Know

ARINC 429 operates at two strictly defined speeds: High Speed (100 kbps) and Low Speed (12.5 kbps). Words are separated by a 4-bit NULL gap (0V differential) to allow receivers to sync.

On The Job

Standard Operating Procedure

Reference Terms

ARINC 429 Transmission Speeds

ARINC 429 operates at two standard speeds: low speed at 12.5–14.5 kbps (kilobits per second) and high speed at 100 kbps.

ARINC 429 High-Speed Rate

Tap to expand definition
ARINC 429 High-Speed Rate

The ARINC 429 high-speed bus operates at 100 kilobits per second (kbps), used for time-critical avionics data such as attitude, navigation, and air data.

ARINC 429 Update Latency

Tap to expand definition
ARINC 429 Update Latency

High latency between data updates on an ARINC 429 bus is caused by either high bus loading (too many labels being transmitted, slowing the update rate for each) or an infrequently updating source LRU — not necessarily a hardware failure.
